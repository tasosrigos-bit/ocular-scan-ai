## Overview
A macular hole is a rare condition in which an opening forms in the macula — the central part of the retina responsible for sharp, straight-ahead vision. It typically results from age-related changes in the vitreous, which naturally shrinks and separates from the retina with age. In some people the vitreous remains more firmly attached to the macula and pulls on it during this process, stretching and eventually tearing the tissue. Macular hole is distinct from age-related macular degeneration, even though both are common in people over 60.

## Symptoms
Early symptoms include distorted or blurry central vision — straight lines may appear bent or wavy, or seem to be missing a piece in the center. Over time, a blind or blurred spot may develop in central vision.

## Risk Factors
Age 60 or older, high myopia (nearsightedness), and prior eye injury or eye surgery increase risk. Having had a macular hole in one eye also raises the risk of developing one in the other eye (about 1 in 10 cases).

## Management
OCT imaging is the standard diagnostic tool for confirming a macular hole. Small or mild cases may be observed rather than treated immediately. When treatment is needed, the standard approach is vitrectomy surgery. Outcomes are notably better with early treatment; delayed treatment increases the risk of permanent central vision loss.

## Referral
Given that early treatment significantly improves outcomes and delayed treatment risks permanent vision loss, a macular hole finding warrants prompt ophthalmologic evaluation rather than routine deferred follow-up.
